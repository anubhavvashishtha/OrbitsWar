================================================================================
           STRATEGIC VULNERABILITY ANALYSIS — TacticalQuadrantAgent
                         (Brutally Honest Edition)
================================================================================

1. QUADRANT RIGIDITY — YOUR FATAL FLAW
--------------------------------------
Your entire strategy is built on a 4-quadrant grid centered on the sun. This
is a geometry that has NOTHING to do with how Orbit Wars actually plays out.
Planets don't respect quadrant boundaries. A planet 1 pixel inside Q0 and a
planet 1 pixel inside Q1 are treated as belonging to entirely different
strategic zones, even though they might be 2 pixels apart. Meanwhile, two
planets at opposite corners of the same quadrant (70+ distance apart) are
"teammates."

An opponent who ignores quadrants and just plays position-based strategy will
out-maneuver you because they see the ACTUAL board, not an artificial overlay.

Your quadrant logic also creates hard seams: planets near boundaries get
sub-optimal orders because they're assigned to the "wrong" quadrant relative
to where the actual fight is.


2. NO FLEET TRACKING / NO INCOMING THREAT ASSESSMENT
-----------------------------------------------------
You only detect attacks AFTER they land (by comparing expected vs actual ship
counts next turn). By then the damage is done. You have zero ability to:

  - See an incoming enemy fleet 10 turns away and pre-reinforce
  - Predict which of your planets will be hit next
  - Abort a doomed attack when enemy reinforcements are en route
  - Dodge or intercept enemy fleets

You have access to obs.fleets. You partially use it to identify
"vulnerable_planet_ids" (planets enemy fleets launched FROM), but you
completely ignore WHERE those fleets are heading. You could trivially
compute fleet trajectories and predict arrival targets. You don't.

Any competent opponent who checks incoming fleets will pre-stack defenses
and you'll waste ships on doomed attacks.


3. ZERO COORDINATION — EVERY PLANET IS AN ISLAND
-------------------------------------------------
Your "shared fleet" logic (Phase 3) is the only multi-planet coordination,
and it's primitive: split ships evenly across all static planets in a
quadrant regardless of distance, timing, or whether the fleet will even
arrive together.

Real coordination means: multiple planets launching fleets that ARRIVE AT
THE SAME TIME on the same target. You never do this. Your attacks are serial
— each planet individually decides to send ships. An opponent with even
basic defensive awareness will pick off your dribbling fleets one by one.


4. ATTACK SIZING IS BROKEN
---------------------------
For attacks, you send: max(min_needed, available - 5% reserve).

This means you almost always send EVERYTHING. The 5% reserve is a joke —
on a planet with 100 ships, you keep 5. One enemy fleet of 6 ships takes
your planet.

You have no concept of:
  - "How many ships do I need to HOLD this planet after capturing it?"
  - "Is this attack even worth it given what I'll lose at home?"
  - "Should I save ships for a bigger coordinated push?"

You're a gambler who goes all-in every hand. Against any opponent who
counter-attacks your emptied planets, you'll hemorrhage territory.


5. NO PRIORITY SYSTEM — YOU ATTACK WHATEVER IS CLOSEST
-------------------------------------------------------
Your target selection is almost entirely "nearest enemy." This is greedy
pathfinding, not strategy. You don't consider:

  - Production value of targets (a radius-5 planet is worth 5x a radius-1)
  - Cost-to-benefit ratio (cheap neutrals vs expensive enemy fortresses)
  - Strategic position (controlling chokepoints, cutting supply lines)
  - Whether you're about to take a planet that 2 other opponents are also
    attacking (wasting ships in a 3-way fight)

A smart opponent will bait you with cheap planets in bad positions while
they grab all the high-production real estate.


6. MOVING PLANET STRATEGY IS ALMOST NONEXISTENT
-------------------------------------------------
Moving planets are your MOST VALUABLE early-game assets (they're close to
the center, often high production), and your logic for them is:

  - If you have no static planets: send moving planet ships to the
    nearest unoccupied static planet. Fine.
  - If you DO have static planets and own the quadrant: attack nearest
    unoccupied moving planet. Okay.
  - Otherwise: attack nearest static planet in same quadrant.

That's it. You have no logic for:
  - Using moving planets as mobile strike platforms
  - Timing attacks based on orbital position
  - Defending moving planets
  - Recognizing that a moving planet will orbit INTO enemy territory

Moving planets rotate. Their strategic value changes every turn based on
where they are. You treat them like static planets that happen to wiggle.


7. PEACETIME BUFFING IS WASTEFUL
---------------------------------
When a quadrant has no enemies, your tanks send production*2 ships to
the nearest moving planet, or production*1 to the weakest small planet.

This is busywork. Those ships are IN TRANSIT and contributing nothing.
If an attack comes during transit, those ships are wasted. Stockpiling
on your highest-production planet (to launch a massive coordinated strike
later) would almost always be better than dribbling ships around during
"peace."


8. NO ENDGAME AWARENESS
-------------------------
You have zero concept of the game timer or score. When time is running out,
the optimal strategy changes dramatically:

  - If you're winning: turtle up, stop attacking, preserve ships
  - If you're losing: go all-in on high-value targets
  - In close games: every ship matters, stop wasting on marginal attacks

You play the exact same way on turn 1 and turn 299. This is a massive
leak against any opponent who adjusts for endgame.


9. REINFORCEMENT LOGIC IS REACTIVE AND DUMB
---------------------------------------------
You detect attacks by noticing ship count drops AFTER combat.
Then you split reinforcement needs evenly across all helpers in the
quadrant.

Problems:
  - The attack already landed. Reinforcements arrive turns later.
  - You don't check if the planet is even worth saving
  - You don't check if reinforcements will arrive in time
  - Equal splitting means distant planets waste ships on long trips
    while nearby planets could have handled it alone
  - You reinforce planets that are about to be hit AGAIN


10. VULNERABLE PLANET EXPLOITATION IS SHALLOW
----------------------------------------------
You identify planets that enemy fleets launched from (vulnerable_planet_ids)
and opportunistically attack them. Good idea. But:

  - You don't track HOW MANY ships left that planet
  - You don't estimate the current garrison
  - You don't time your attack to arrive when the planet is weakest
  - You attack them with the same nearest-first greedy logic

A real exploit would be: "Planet X just sent 80% of its ships away.
I can calculate exactly how many it has now. I know my fleet will arrive
in N turns during which it produces M ships. I need exactly M + remaining
+ 1 ships." You don't do any of this math.


11. NO MULTI-PLAYER AWARENESS
------------------------------
This is a 4-player game. You treat every non-you player identically.
You don't consider:

  - Who is winning (and therefore the biggest threat)
  - Who is fighting whom (let them weaken each other)
  - Temporary alliances of convenience (don't attack the player who's
    keeping your rival busy)
  - Third-party interference (don't attack a planet that Player 3 is
    about to take — let them waste ships, then take it from them)

You're playing 3 separate 1v1s simultaneously instead of one 4-player game.


12. SUN AVOIDANCE IS BINARY — NO WAYPOINT ROUTING
--------------------------------------------------
If a direct path to a target hits the sun, you return None (for static
planets) or fall back to intercept search. You never consider:

  - Sending to an intermediate planet first, then forwarding
  - Choosing an angle that arcs around the sun (fleet paths are straight,
    but you could choose a relay point)
  - Whether the planet will rotate to a sun-safe angle soon

You just give up on anything behind the sun. Opponents on the far side
of the board are effectively immune to you until orbital mechanics
accidentally clear a path.


13. PRODUCTION VALUE IS IGNORED IN TARGETING
---------------------------------------------
You never factor planet.production into attack decisions. A production-5
planet defended by 20 ships is VASTLY more valuable than a production-1
planet defended by 5 ships. But your agent treats the 5-ship planet as
the better target because it's "easier."

Over 300 turns, production compounds. Ignoring it means you grab junk
planets while opponents grab powerhouses.


14. _targeted DICT LEAKS STALE STATE
--------------------------------------
You mark planets as "targeted" with an expiry of current_turn + turns + 2.
But:
  - If your fleet gets destroyed by the sun or combat, the target stays
    locked until expiry — nobody else attacks it
  - If the target changes owner before your fleet arrives, you don't
    re-evaluate
  - Multiple planets might need to attack the same target for
    coordinated assaults, but _targeted prevents this

The targeting lock is a blunt instrument that causes missed opportunities.


15. NO COMET EXPLOITATION
--------------------------
You explicitly FILTER OUT comets: planets = [p for p in all_planets
if p.id not in comet_ids]. Comets are temporary planets flying through
the board. They can be:

  - Free ship sources if neutral and weakly defended
  - Mobile strike platforms if captured
  - Threats if captured by enemies

You completely ignore them. Any opponent who grabs a comet passing
through your territory gets a free forward base.


================================================================================
SUMMARY: Your agent is a decent "expand to nearest, defend reactively"
bot that will beat random/do-nothing opponents. But against any opponent
with fleet tracking, coordinated attacks, production-weighted targeting,
or endgame awareness, you will lose. The quadrant system creates artificial
blindness, the all-in attack sizing leaves you perpetually exposed, and
the complete absence of incoming fleet prediction means you're always
reacting one turn too late.

The single most impactful fix: TRACK INCOMING FLEETS. Know what's coming,
when it's arriving, and where. Everything else flows from that.
================================================================================
